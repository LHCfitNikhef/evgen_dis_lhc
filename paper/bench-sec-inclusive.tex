\section{Event generation for inclusive DIS}
\label{sec:inclusive}
\label{sec:results}

We consider first the results of event generator benchmarking for inclusive DIS, where only the fiducial cuts of Eq.~(\ref{eq:fiducial}) are applied, and compare predictions both for total cross-sections and for differential distributions in the DIS kinematic variables.
%
Subsequently we consider charm-tagged cross-sections (Sect.~\ref{sec:charm}), observables dependent on the final-state hadronic system for the FASER$\nu$ acceptance (Sect.~\ref{sec:hadronic}), and applications to FASER where cross-sections are weighted by the LHC neutrino fluxes (Sect.~\ref{sec:faser-app}). 

\paragraph{Total fiducial cross-sections.}
\label{sec:incl-nlo}
\label{sec:incl-scales}
%
Fig.~\ref{fig:sigma-vs-energy} displays the inclusive per-nucleon cross-section in the fiducial region  Eq.~(\ref{eq:fiducial}) evaluated  on a tungsten target as a function of the beam energy for muon  neutral-current and muon-neutrino charged-current DIS.
%
We provide results for three NLO-matched event generators, \sherpa, \herwig, and \powheg, as well as for the analytical structure-function calculations from \yadism at NLO and NNLO.
%
MHOUs are evaluated from the \yadism prediction at each order, in the same scheme as the corresponding central curve.
%
At NLO, \yadism predictions are provided in the ZM-VFNS and FONLL schemes, so that their difference estimates the impact of charm mass effects in the total cross-section.
%
At NNLO, we use FONLL for neutral-current and ZM-VFNS for charged-current scattering, since in the latter case the massive coefficient functions, although known at NNLO~\cite{Berger:2016inr,Gao:2017kkx,Caola:2026kvl}, are not yet implemented in \yadism. 
%
The bottom panels display the ratio of the various calculations to the 
    massless NLO \yadism calculation.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{figure}[!tbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figures/pp_sigma_vs_energy.png}
  \caption{Inclusive fiducial cross-section per nucleon on a tungsten target as a function of the beam energy for muon $(\mu^-)$ neutral-current (left) and muon-neutrino ($\nu_\mu$) charged-current (right panel) scattering in the fiducial region defined by Eq.~(\ref{eq:fiducial}).
%
We provide results for three NLO-matched event generators (\sherpa, \herwig, and \powheg) as well as for the analytical calculations from \yadism at NLO (ZM-VFNS and FONLL) and at NNLO (FONLL for NC and ZM-VFNS for CC).
%
The bottom panels display the ratio to the massless NLO \yadism calculation.
%
The error bands in the generator predictions correspond to the MC statistical uncertainties.
    }
  \label{fig:sigma-vs-energy}
\end{figure}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The total fiducial rates predicted by the three NLO-matched generators agree among themselves and with the \yadism reference at the $\lsim 2\%$ level.
%
Residual differences are covered by the MC statistics, except for \powhegres, which lies $1$--$2\%$ below the reference for muon DIS.
%
NNLO corrections range between $-4\%$ and $-7\%$ for muon DIS and are around $-1.5\%$ for neutrino DIS; for both currents the NLO and NNLO MHOU bands overlap.
%
MHOUs being larger for NC as compared to CC is partially explained by the fact that the former probe smaller values of $Q^2$ due to the $Q^{-4}$ enhancement of the virtual photon propagator. 
%
Raising the momentum-transfer cut to $Q^2 > 11$~GeV$^2$ roughly halves the MHOUs in NC DIS, although they remain larger than in CC.
%
Charm mass effects, as estimated by the \yadism FONLL calculation, are negligible at the level of inclusive fiducial cross-sections. 
%
The agreement between the three generators and the \yadism reference shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:sigma-vs-energy}, despite the use of different parton shower algorithms and hadronisation models, confirms that fiducial cross-sections are fully determined by the DIS matrix elements, which are identical in all the massless NLO calculations considered.

\paragraph{Differential distributions.}
\label{sec:incl-diff}
%
Fig.~\ref{fig:dis-distributions} displays the same benchmark comparison between the predictions of NLO-matched event generators and analytical calculations as in Fig.~\ref{fig:sigma-vs-energy} for differential distributions in the same fiducial region.
%
For illustration purposes we show $E_\ell=1$ TeV as a
representative energy; we have verified that the results are qualitatively similar at other incoming lepton energies.
%
As in Fig.~\ref{fig:sigma-vs-energy}, we quote the differential cross-sections per nucleon on a tungsten target, whose integral reproduces the values of Fig.~\ref{fig:sigma-vs-energy}.
%
Error bars in the generator predictions are the  Monte Carlo integration statistics.
%
The bottom panels show the ratio to the NLO massless \yadism reference calculation.

From top to bottom, Fig.~\ref{fig:dis-distributions} displays the distributions in Bjorken $x_{\rm Bj}$, the scattered-lepton energy $E_{\ell'}$ and polar angle $\theta_{\ell'}$, and the momentum transfer $Q^2$, in all cases in the target rest frame.
%
Note that these distributions are not all independent, since for a fixed beam energy the scattering kinematics only has two degrees of freedom.
%
For instance, $Q^2 = 2 E_\ell E_{\ell'} (1 - \cos\theta_{\ell'})$.
%
For this reason, the hadronic energy $E_h$ and inelasticity $y$ distributions are not shown, since for fixed $E_\ell$ they are both linear in $E_{\ell'}$, hence comparing the different calculations for the latter is sufficient. 
%
These are observables that the  analytic \yadism calculation can predict, since they are built from the leptonic kinematics without reference to the hadronic final state.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{figure}[!tbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.96\textwidth]{figures/pp_dis_distributions.png}
  \caption{Same as Fig.~\ref{fig:sigma-vs-energy} for differential distributions in the fiducial region at $E_\ell=1$ TeV.
%
From top to bottom, we show the distributions in Bjorken $x_{\rm Bj}$, the scattered-lepton
    energy $E_{\ell'}$ and polar angle $\theta_{\ell'}$, and the momentum transfer $Q^2$, evaluated in the target rest frame.
    %
    Error bars indicate  the  Monte Carlo integration statistics.}
  \label{fig:dis-distributions}
\end{figure}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The main result of Fig.~\ref{fig:dis-distributions} is the excellent agreement between the NLO generators and the analytical \yadism ZM reference for both currents, echoing that of the  integrated cross-sections.
%
Residual differences are at the per-cent level, are consistent with the MC statistical fluctuations, and overwhelmingly covered by the NLO MHOU band. 
%
As for the fiducial cross-sections, this agreement is reassuring since DIS distributions should not be sensitive to the modelling of the hadronic final state and depend only on the NLO partonic matrix element, all other physical inputs kept the same.

From Fig.~\ref{fig:dis-distributions} we also find that, as was the case for inclusive cross-sections, NNLO corrections can be sizeable for NC scattering across the whole phase space.
%
For CC scattering, NNLO corrections can be considerably larger than at the inclusive level, reaching for example $-10\%$ at small $x_{\rm Bj}$ and $-6\%$ at small $Q^2$.
%
This result strengthens the need for DIS event generators reaching NNLO accuracy to ensure the full scientific exploitation of the FASER and SND@LHC far-forward physics programme.
%
Charm-mass effects, on the other hand, remain negligible also at the level of differential distributions, and in particular are always smaller than the spread between generators.
%
We conclude that for inclusive and differential DIS distributions, purely massless calculations should be reliable for the FASER kinematics.

\paragraph{Comparison with \genie.}
%
Having established that three NLO-matched event generators agree with the structure-function calculation for a common set of physics settings, we move to discuss the comparison with \genie, the baseline generator used in FASER.
%
Fig.~\ref{fig:genie-vs-energy} displays, in the same format as Fig.~\ref{fig:sigma-vs-energy}, a comparison of the total inclusive cross-sections predicted by \yadism NLO in the FONLL general-mass scheme with \powheg and different \genie tunes.
 %
 In the left panel, we show predictions with the \genie~{\tt G18\_02a} tune (LO matrix elements in the Bodek--Yang model) and GRV98LO and NNPDF4.0 NNLO as PDF sets.
 %
 In the right panel, we show the same LO tune and in addition the NLO {\tt GHE19\_00a} tune with {\sc\small HEDIS}, which evaluates NLO structure functions with \apfel. 
  %
  The lower panels display the ratio to the \yadism FONLL reference.
  %
  In all panels, the \yadism MHOUs are displayed.
  %
  For the case of the {\tt G18\_02a} tune we stop the energy scan at $E_\ell=1$ TeV, its declared limit of validity.\footnote{Within our pipeline it is possible to generate \genie splines extending to higher values of $E_\ell$, should the user require it.} 
%
The {\tt G18\_02a} tune of \genie with GRV98LO as input PDF is the current baseline calculation used in FASER data analysis. 
%
We use \yadism FONLL as reference since this \genie cross-section model also accounts for the charm mass effects. 

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\begin{figure}[!tbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figures/pp_genie_vs_energy.png}
  \caption{Same as Fig.~\ref{fig:sigma-vs-energy}
 comparing the total inclusive cross-sections predicted by \yadism NLO in the FONLL scheme with \powhegres (muon DIS) and \powhegtwo (neutrino DIS) and different \genie tunes: GRV98LO (default), same with NNPDF4.0, and the {\sc\small HEDIS} tune. 
  %
  In the right panel we also display the  \yadism FONLL prediction evaluated with the same PDF set as {\sc\small HEDIS} uses, namely {\tt NNPDF31sx\_nlo\_as\_0118\_LHCb\_nf\_6}.
  %
  The lower panels display the ratio to the \yadism FONLL reference.
  %
  The default \genie tune stops at $E_\ell=1$ TeV, its declared limit of validity. 
 }
  \label{fig:genie-vs-energy}
\end{figure}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

From Fig.~\ref{fig:genie-vs-energy} we observe that the default \genie tune agrees with both NLO calculations (\yadism and \powheg) due to an accidental cancellation of competing effects, namely the PDF and the matrix-element model.
%
For muon DIS, using in \genie the same PDF set as the NLO calculations (NNPDF4.0) results in a cross-section up to 6\% higher than the \yadism NLO reference.
%
For neutrino DIS a similar effect is found, and there the NLO tune based on {\sc\small HEDIS} predicts a suppression of the inclusive rates by 5\% in the considered $E_\nu$ range.
%
This last offset is entirely a PDF effect.
%
Indeed, {\tt GHE19\_00a} evaluates its structure functions with {\tt NNPDF31sx\_nlo\_as\_0118\_LHCb\_nf\_6}~\cite{Ball:2017otu,Bertone:2018dse,NNPDF:2017mvq}, whose light-quark densities lie below those of \nnpdf over the $x$ range that dominates the rate.\footnote{This PDF is tailored for applications to ultra-high-energy (UHE) neutrino astronomy, with $D$-meson data constraining its small-$x$ gluon PDF.} 
%
When the \yadism FONLL calculation is evaluated with the same PDF set, the agreement with the  {\sc\small HEDIS} predictions is excellent. 
%
From the assessment of Fig.~\ref{fig:genie-vs-energy} one can infer that, while the default tune of \genie reproduces the predictions of NLO QCD calculations with modern PDFs, this agreement will not extend to differential observables due to its accidental nature.

To illustrate this,  Fig.~\ref{fig:genie-dis-distributions} compares the same differential distributions of Fig.~\ref{fig:dis-distributions} between \yadism, \powheg, and the \genie tunes as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:genie-vs-energy}.
%
As opposed to the inclusive case, differences between the default \genie tune (with GRV98LO as input PDF) and the NLO QCD calculations (\yadism and \powheg, which agree with each other for all observables) can be significant.
%
For example, these differences range between $-16\%$ and $+22\%$ ($-15\%$ and $+8\%$) for the $x_{\rm Bj}$ distribution in muon (neutrino) DIS, and up to $-6\%$ ($-10\%$) for the corresponding $\theta_{\ell'}$ distributions.
%
Furthermore, these differences are in general well outside the MHOU band of the NLO calculation, and are removed neither by changing the PDF to NNPDF4.0 in \genie nor by changing the tune to {\sc\small HEDIS}. 
%
One concludes that using \genie instead of NLO QCD calculations could introduce a systematic modelling bias in the analysis and interpretation of muon and neutrino DIS at FASER and SND@LHC.

In Sect.~\ref{sec:hadronic} we revisit the comparison for observables at the level of the FASER$\nu$ selection, and in Sect.~\ref{sec:faser-app} with the cross-sections weighted by the energy-dependent LHC neutrino fluxes.
